How Lagrange Multipliers Restore Your Wallet

This helper gives you the exact multipliers (λ) needed to recombine your paper shares. Multiply each encrypted value (Y) by its multiplier, sum the results, and reduce modulo 2053 to recover a row.

  1. Record the share numbers (the X printed on each share) for the K shares you possess.
  2. Use the multipliers to compute Recovered = Σ(Y × λ) mod 2053 for each word and checksum row.
  3. Repeat for every row and finally verify the Global Integrity Check (GIC) to confirm the recovery.

After every row, add the three recovered word numbers plus the row number (1 for the first row, 2 for the second, and so on), modulo 2053, and confirm the result matches that row's checksum. Verify the three column checksums (CC1, CC2, CC3) by summing each column's words plus 10, 20, or 30 respectively. Finally, verify the GIC value in the bottom-right table cell before rebuilding the mnemonic.

How to Use This Tool

This tool helps you perform DuraShare manually with pencil and paper by providing the necessary secure random numbers.

  1. Get your BIP39 word indices: Find your 12 or 24 secret words on the official BIP39 English wordlist. You need the number (index) for each word. Our tool uses a one-indexed list (the first word, "abandon", is 1; the last word is 2048). Be careful: some lists and programming APIs are zero-indexed (abandon = 0); if you use those, add 1 before plugging the value into the polynomial. Pro tip: paste the wordlist into a spreadsheet and number it starting from 1.
  2. Calculate Point Coordinates: For each of your secret word indices, use the polynomial equation shown above to calculate a "share" value (Y). The 'X' in the equation is the share number (e.g., 1 for the first share, 2 for the second, and so on).
  3. All calculations are modulo 2053. This is a prime number. To perform a "modulo" operation, you divide your result by 2053 and take the remainder. For example, 2055 mod 2053 is 2.
